Festa do Avante is the largest single event the Margem Sul holds each year, and in 2026 it runs **4, 5 and 6 September** at Quinta da Atalaia, in Amora, in the Seixal concelho.

Three days, a permanent site of tens of hectares, dozens of stages and food houses, and an audience that comes from the whole country. If you live in the Margem Sul, it's the weekend the trains arrive full and the bridges stop moving on Sunday night. If you've never been, this is the minimum worth knowing before buying a ticket.

## The essentials

**Dates.** Friday 4, Saturday 5 and Sunday 6 September 2026.

**Hours.** Friday 6pm to 1.30am. Saturday 10am to 1.30am. Sunday 10am to 11pm.

**Entry.** The entrada permanente — the EP, as everyone calls it — costs €35 bought before 3 September and covers all three days. It isn't a day ticket; it's a pass to the whole festival.

**Where.** Quinta da Atalaia, Av. Baía Natural do Seixal 415, 2845-606 Amora. The site belongs to the organisers and is the same every year, which is why it has grown trees, named streets and real infrastructure rather than a rented field.

## Getting there without a car

This is the part that separates people who have been from people going for the first time. On Sunday night, with tens of thousands leaving at once, the car is the worst option available.

**By ferry.** Cais do Sodré to Seixal, around 25 minutes on the water, then a bus from the river terminal. It's the best arrival — you come in across the bay with the north bank pulling away behind you — and it avoids the bridge entirely.

**By train.** Fertagus from Roma-Areeiro or Sete Rios to Foros de Amora or Corroios, then a bus. The line crosses the Ponte 25 de Abril on the deck below the road.

Extra services and dedicated shuttles run on festival days. The full transport plan is published on the official site the week before — worth checking before you leave, because it changes from year to year.

## What to expect inside

The site is genuinely large. You don't cross it in a quarter of an hour and you won't see all of it in a day. Stages are spread across the ground, one of them big enough for international headliners, the rest distributed through auditoriums, tents and open-air spaces.

The food is central rather than incidental. The tasquinhas are organised by region of the country and by organisation, each with its own kitchen, and it's entirely possible to spend the festival eating something different every time. Prices are low for a festival.

There's a bookshop, exhibitions, sport, spaces for children, and a campsite inside the grounds with advance registration that closes on 3 September.

## The honest part

Festa do Avante is the festival of the Portuguese Communist Party. It isn't a music festival that happens to have a party behind it: the political programme — rallies, debates, the Sunday closing — is the spine of the event, and it's visible everywhere on the site.

That isn't a warning, it's information. Plenty of people go for the concerts and the food, and they're welcome. But if you buy a ticket expecting a conventional summer festival, you'll be somewhere other than you pictured. Better to know first.

What isn't in question is the scale. Three days, a purpose-built site developed over decades, and more people than any other event the Margem Sul receives. For those who live here, it's the weekend the region stops being the other side of the river and becomes the destination.

## Before you go

- Buy the EP before 3 September or pay more.
- If you're camping, register by the same date.
- Take cash for the tasquinhas, even though there are cash machines on site.
- Comfortable shoes: the ground is packed earth and you'll walk kilometres.
- Check the transport plan the week before. On Sunday night, the ferry and the train beat the car comfortably.
