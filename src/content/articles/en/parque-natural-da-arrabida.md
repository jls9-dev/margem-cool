The Parque Natural da Arrábida is the reason these hills are still what they are. It was created on **28 July 1976**, by Decreto-Lei 622/76, covering around **10,800 hectares** across the concelhos of **Setúbal, Palmela and Sesimbra**.

Without it, the most beautiful stretch of coast in metropolitan Lisbon would have met the fate of almost all Portuguese coast this close to a capital.

## What it protects

**On land**, a stand of **Mediterranean maquis** rare at a European scale: kermes oak, strawberry tree, wild olive and mastic, on limestone soil and facing south. It is vegetation adapted to heat and drought, and it survived here because the hills are too steep to plough.

Birds of prey nest on the cliffs, and the geology itself — Jurassic limestone lifted and folded — is part of what is protected.

**At sea**, since **1998**, the **Parque Marinho Professor Luiz Saldanha**: around 53 kilometres of coast between the hills and Cabo Espichel, with rocky seabeds of a diversity unusual on the Portuguese coast. It has zones with different levels of restriction on fishing and diving.

## What that means for visitors

**There is no entry fee.** The park is open territory, with public roads and freely accessible beaches. You pay for parking where it exists.

**The car does not get through in summer.** From 4 June to 15 September, 7am to 8pm, the beach roads are closed to private cars, with reinforced buses as the alternative. The full rules are in the [Arrábida beaches guide](/en/guides/praias-da-arrabida/).

**No camping outside the designated site** and no fires. There is a campsite at Creiro.

**Take your rubbish home.** In hills under this much visitor pressure in August, the difference is visible.

## What is inside

The **beaches** — [Figueirinha](/en/guides/praia-da-figueirinha/), Galapos, [Galapinhos](/en/guides/praia-dos-galapinhos/), Portinho, Creiro and [Albarquel](/en/guides/praia-de-albarquel/).

The **Convento da Arrábida**, sixteenth-century, on the slope above the Portinho, with restricted visiting.

The **hill road** between Setúbal and the Portinho, with viewpoints along the way — the easiest way to grasp the scale of the thing.

And **Cabo Espichel** at the western end, which closes the marine park and has [its own guide](/en/guides/cabo-espichel/).

## The honest part

The park has a structural problem: it is small, it is close to three million people, and its beaches are among the best in the country. In August that is a daily tension between conservation and access, and the traffic restriction is the attempt to manage it.

Anyone visiting outside August finds different hills — the same landscape, without the queue. September, after the 15th, is the best moment of the year.
