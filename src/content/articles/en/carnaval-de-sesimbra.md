Carnaval de Sesimbra is one of the oldest and most-watched in the Margem Sul, and in 2026 it runs **12 to 21 February**. It isn't just a weekend of parades: it's ten days spread across Sesimbra town, Quinta do Conde and several of the concelho's smaller freguesias, each with its own piece of the tradition.

## The essentials

**Dates.** Thursday 12 to Friday 21 February 2026. The busiest stretch runs Saturday 14 to Tuesday 17, with the Enterro do Bacalhau officially closing the festival on Wednesday 18.

**Where.** The main parades run down Avenida 25 de Abril in Sesimbra town. Quinta do Conde, the concelho's most populous freguesia, has its own parade starting from the Largo da Junta de Freguesia. The cegadas — the part that's distinctly Sesimbra's own — happen across Pedreiras, Aldeia do Meco, Azoia, Zambujal de Baixo and Alfarim.

**Grandstands.** There are two grandstands with 600 seated places on Avenida 25 de Abril, sold as individual tickets, family packages and an all-parades pass. The council hadn't published prices for these at the time of writing — check the event's official site nearer the date if you want a seat.

## The cegadas, the part you won't see anywhere else

If the only Carnaval you know is samba schools and confetti, the cegadas are the reason to come to Sesimbra specifically. They're small plays sung in verse, performed by groups made up exclusively of men, with satirical lyrics about the year's events and public figures, accompanied by Portuguese guitar. It's a century-old custom — dating to the late 19th century — and Sesimbra is one of the few concelhos in the country where it's still practised regularly, largely thanks to the groups from Alfarim and Zambujal.

There's no ticket and no grandstand: the cegadas happen in the street, in the squares and churchyards of the freguesias themselves, and the 2026 programme spreads sessions across six different days — worth choosing your freguesia by which day you go.

## The programme, day by day

**Thursday 12.** Parade of educational establishments, 10.30am, on Avenida 25 de Abril.

**Friday 13.** School parade in Quinta do Conde, 10.30am, on Avenida 1.º de Maio.

**Saturday 14.** The busiest single day: children's costume competition and cegadas at the Fortaleza de Santiago from 11am, the Quinta do Conde parade at 3pm, a women's afro-axé group through Sesimbra's streets at 4pm, and cegadas in Pedreiras and Aldeia do Meco in the late afternoon.

**Sunday 15.** Samba schools parade at 2.30pm, plus cegadas in Azoia, Zambujal de Baixo and Alfarim.

**Monday 16.** Clown-costume procession at 3pm — the day built for children, with the Comboio da Alegria run by the Junta de Freguesia de Santiago for anyone with reduced mobility.

**Tuesday 17.** Another samba-schools parade at 2.30pm, and the cavalhadas — the traditional horseback procession — in Alfarim at 3pm.

**Wednesday 18.** The Enterro do Bacalhau, 9.30pm, through the streets of the centre. It's the procession that declares Carnaval officially over.

**Friday 21.** One last session of cegadas with traditional dances, at the Cineteatro Municipal João Mota, 4pm and 9.30pm — the echo after the street has already emptied.

## Getting there

Sesimbra has no train line — you get there by bus or by car. The Carris Metropolitana 3721 route connects Lisbon (Sete Rios) to Sesimbra year-round. On Carnaval days the council usually reinforces the timetable and runs a free shuttle, but the specific plan for 2026 hadn't been published at the time of writing — check the event's official site the week before.

By car, it's the A2 and then the road into Sesimbra. Parking in the centre gets tight on the busiest parade days, so factor that in if you're coming from outside.

## The honest bit

Carnaval de Sesimbra doesn't have Rio's scale or the polished choreography of Portugal's bigger carnivals. What it has is ten days spread across a whole concelho rather than concentrated on one avenue, and a tradition — the cegadas — that barely survives anywhere else. Turn up just for Saturday's parade and you get half the experience. Spread the visit across two or three different days, including at least one session of cegadas in one of the smaller freguesias, and you see the difference between a city Carnaval and a concelho one.
