Castelo de Palmela sits on a spur above the town, and the reason to climb it is not the fortification itself — it is what happened inside it for four hundred years.

It was the **definitive seat of the Military Order of Santiago**, from 1443 until the Order was dissolved in 1834. Its members — *freires* in peacetime, knights in war — lived here, and the castle's symbol is still the sword-shaped cross that identified them.

## What is inside the walls

**The convent**, today a **pousada**: the rooms occupy the old cells and the restaurant what was the Order's refectory. It is one of the few ways to sleep inside a national monument in Portugal.

**The Igreja de Santiago**, whose sacristy houses the study centre on the Order of Santiago.

**The ruins of the Igreja de Santa Maria**, and the **keep**, from which you see the rest.

**The Praça de Armas** and the military signals station, which give the measure of what this was as a defensive position.

## The view

This is the argument nobody disputes. From the top you see **half the Setúbal peninsula**: the Serra da Arrábida to the south, the plain and the vineyards to the east, the Sado estuary, and on a clear day Lisbon to the north.

Palmela occupies the highest point between the peninsula's two shores, and it is immediately obvious why a military order settled here rather than anywhere else.

## Hours — the honest part

**We are not going to give you exact times**, because we cannot verify them against a source we can re-read. The spaces inside the castle — grounds, keep, Igreja de Santiago, museum core — do not all open at once and the hours change through the year.

If your visit depends on it, confirm with the **Palmela tourism office**: **212 332 122** or **turismo@cm-palmela.pt**. They also arrange guided visits.

We would rather say that than publish a timetable that looks official and isn't.

## When to go

September, during the **[Festas das Vindimas](/en/places/palmela/)**, is when the town is most alive — the blessing of the grapes, the procession, and the castle in the middle of it.

Otherwise, late in the day: the westward view takes in the hills and the sun sets behind them.

## The honest part

The castle is a large and uneven ensemble: parts restored and in use, parts held as consolidated ruin, a four-star pousada and a public viewpoint, all inside the same walls.

That bothers anyone looking for a coherent monument and delights anyone who likes places with layers. It is honestly both.
