From May to September the Margem Sul is nearly always in festa somewhere. That is not a figure of speech: nine concelhos, dozens of freguesias, and each with its own saint, its own festa committee and its own weekend fixed decades ago.

For anyone arriving from outside this is invisible until it happens — and then it is a closed street, a stage going up and the smell of sardines at eleven o'clock on a Tuesday night. This guide is the map of the season.

## How a popular festa works

Nearly all follow the same shape. The organiser is the parish council, a local association, or a festa committee elected for the purpose. The occasion is the patron saint. It runs two to four days, always taking in a weekend.

The ground is the street or the square, with a stage at one end and tasquinhas around it. The tasquinhas are the serious part: each is run by a group — the firefighters, the folk group, the football club — and the money stays with the association. You eat snails, bifanas, pork, sardines in season, and drink beer and house wine.

**There is no admission.** You pay for what you consume. That is the essential difference between a popular festa and a festival.

## The calendar, month by month

### May — the season opens

**Feira de Maio, Alcochete.** One of the oldest fairs in the region, with roots in the tradition of free fairs. It takes over the town for days.

**Festas do Senhor Jesus das Chagas, Sesimbra.** The fishing town's great festa, with a procession involving the sea and the fishing community. It is Sesimbra's festa, not a festa in Sesimbra, and the difference shows.

### June — the santos populares

The month of the arraiais. Santo António on the 13th, São João on the 24th, São Pedro on the 29th — and around those dates nearly every freguesia puts up its own. It is the easiest time to stumble into a festa you were not looking for.

Grilled sardines, basil plants, live music and streets strung with paper. In the riverside freguesias, São Pedro carries extra weight as patron of fishermen.

### July — the big fairs

**Feira de Sant'Iago, Setúbal.** The city's largest annual fair, on its own ground, between concerts, rides, craft stalls and tasquinhas. It runs for weeks and it marks the Setúbal summer.

### August — the fullest month

**Festas do Barreiro**, 14th to 23rd, in honour of Nossa Senhora do Rosário. The procession goes **by land and by sea** — in a city built on its relationship with the Tagus, that is the gesture that defines it.

**Nossa Senhora da Atalaia, Montijo.** The oldest romaria in southern Portugal, from a vow made in 1507, with five processions still converging on Atalaia from Palmela, Sesimbra and Montijo itself.

**Barrete Verde e das Salinas, Alcochete**, with its bull-running and campino tradition.

And parish festas overlapping across the whole region, weekend after weekend. The rule of thumb: if you hear music in the distance on an August night, there is a festa less than two kilometres away.

### September — the close

**Festas das Vindimas, Palmela.** The wine town's festa, with the blessing of the grapes, treading and a procession.

**Nossa Senhora da Boa Viagem, Moita.** Ten days, with a procession that blesses the traditional Tagus boats — the same fragatas and varinos the concelho was built on — and the Noite do Fragateiro with marchas populares.

**Festa do Avante, Seixal.** 4th to 6th September 2026, at Quinta da Atalaia. The largest gathering the Margem Sul receives all year, and the only one on this list with a ticket. It has [its own guide](/en/guides/festa-do-avante-2026/).

## How to find the exact dates

Here is the honest part: **the dates move every year and few are published far ahead.** Most parish festas confirm their programme two or three weeks before.

Where they appear first:

- The **junta de freguesia** website and Facebook page — almost always the first to publish the poster.
- The **câmara municipal** cultural listings for the concelho.
- The paper posters taped up in cafés and at bus stops, which remain, without irony, the most reliable source for the small festas.
