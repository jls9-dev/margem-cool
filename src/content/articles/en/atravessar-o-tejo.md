Anyone who lives on the Margem Sul and works in Lisbon asks this question more often than they'd like: train, ferry or car? The right answer isn't the same for everyone — it depends where you live, where you're going, and what time you leave the house.

This guide puts the three options side by side, with what each one actually serves.

## The train: Fertagus

Fertagus is the line that crosses Ponte 25 de Abril on its own track, below the road deck, connecting Setúbal to Lisbon. Fourteen stations, south to north: **Setúbal, Palmela, Venda do Alcaide, Pinhal Novo, Penalva, Coina, Fogueteiro, Foros de Amora, Corroios, Pragal, Campolide, Sete Rios, Entrecampos and Roma-Areeiro**.

The full journey, from Setúbal to the central Lisbon stations, takes about 50 minutes. Anyone boarding closer to the bridge — Corroios, Pragal — is across in considerably less time.

On weekdays, trains run roughly every 20 minutes during the morning and early-evening peaks; the gap widens across the rest of the day and at weekends. Payment is by a Navegante card loaded with zones, or directly with a contactless bank card (EMV) at the validators — there's no need to buy a ticket in advance.

### Who Fertagus serves best

It serves well anyone living near one of its stations — Corroios, Foros de Amora and Pragal especially, the ones closest to the bridge — whose Lisbon destination is near Entrecampos, Sete Rios or Roma-Areeiro. For the Baixa or Chiado, it still means changing onto the Metro at one of those stations.

## The ferries: five routes, five different realities

Transtejo Soflusa runs five ferry routes between the Margem Sul and Lisbon, and they aren't interchangeable — each serves a different area and lands somewhere different on the other side.

### Cacilhas – Cais do Sodré

The shortest and most frequent: a few minutes of water, landing at Cais do Sodré, where the Metro's green line starts. A single ticket costs **€2.00**, or **€1.60** paying by zapping with a loaded Navegante or Viva Viagem card. It's the natural crossing for anyone living in Almada, Cova da Piedade or Cacilhas itself.

### Seixal – Cais do Sodré

Connects Seixal's town centre directly to Cais do Sodré, without going via Cacilhas. A single ticket costs **€2.85**. For anyone living in the concelho seat, away from a Fertagus station, it's the most direct route.

### Barreiro – Terreiro do Paço

Barreiro's long-standing crossing, at **€2.95**, lands at Terreiro do Paço, where the Metro's blue line starts. Serves Barreiro itself and the nearby freguesias, including Coina and Palhais.

### Montijo – Cais do Sodré

The longest of the five, at **€3.25**, for anyone living on the other side of the estuary — outside the traditional core of the Margem Sul but working the same river-crossing logic.

### Trafaria – Porto Brandão – Belém

The cheapest, at **€1.50**, and the only one that doesn't land in central Lisbon — it terminates at Belém, which makes it more useful for anyone working there than for anyone heading into the Baixa.

These five fares were confirmed on Transtejo Soflusa's own tariff page on 21 September 2026. Public transport prices change; if your trip depends on the exact figure, check before you leave.

## By car: the bridges

Ponte 25 de Abril and Ponte Vasco da Gama are the obvious alternative for anyone without a train station or a ferry landing near home, or who simply needs the car at the other end.

The toll applies only in the Lisbon direction — you leave the Margem Sul free, and pay coming in. The current price is published on the Lusoponte site, which changes with inflation every year; we haven't repeated it here so this page doesn't go stale within months.

What stays stable, year after year, is the traffic pattern: the queue for the bridge builds up in a predictable way during the morning and early-evening peaks. Anyone with a train or ferry option nearly always saves time by leaving the car at home during those hours, even accounting for the toll they'd otherwise pay.

## Which to choose, by where you live

- **Almada, Cova da Piedade, Cacilhas** — the ferry to Cais do Sodré is the most direct option; anyone closer to Pragal also has Fertagus.
- **Corroios, Amora** — Fertagus is the natural choice, with stations in both freguesias.
- **Seixal (the town centre) and the rest of the concelho** — the direct Seixal–Cais do Sodré ferry, no changing.
- **Barreiro, Coina, Palhais** — the Barreiro–Terreiro do Paço ferry, connecting to the Metro's blue line.
- **Setúbal, Palmela** — Fertagus, as the end-to-end option; the journey is longer, but there's no direct river crossing to Lisbon.
- **Montijo** — the ferry to Cais do Sodré is the river option; by car, Ponte Vasco da Gama.

## The honest bit

No single option wins every time. Anyone who actually times their door-to-door journey usually finds the gap between options is minutes, not half an hour — and that the real decision rests more on where home and work happen to sit than on which mode is "best" in the abstract. The useful question isn't which crossing is fastest on the Margem Sul; it's which one is fastest for your own route.
