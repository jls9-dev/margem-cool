Lagoa de Albufeira is a coastal lagoon in the Sesimbra concelho, separated from the Atlantic by a bar of sand. Which means that within a couple of hundred metres you have two entirely different bodies of water: on one side a still, shallow, tepid surface; on the other, open Atlantic with real swell.

There is nothing else like it in the Margem Sul, which is why the place comes up in conversation far more often than its size would suggest.

## The two sides

**The lagoon.** Water with almost no swell, shallow a long way out, and noticeably warmer than the sea because it sits still in the sun. It's where people with small children go, and it's also where the kitesurfing and windsurfing happen — flat water with wind is exactly what those sports want.

**The sea.** On the far side of the sand bar it's the Atlantic, with everything that implies: waves, currents and cold water. It's a few minutes' walk from the lagoon, which allows the rare trick of changing the kind of day you're having without changing where you are.

## The bar

The sand bar separating the lagoon from the sea tends to close over time. Periodically it is **opened**, letting salt water in to renew the lagoon — which is what keeps the water quality and the ecosystem working.

Two things worth knowing about this. First: when the bar is open there is a **current** in the channel between lagoon and sea, and it can be strong. It isn't a place to leave anyone floating unattended.

Second: the lagoon looks different depending on the state of the bar. If you remember it from another year and find it changed, that's most likely why.

## When to go

As everywhere on this coast, September is the best month: the water at its warmest point of the year, the wind dropping, and a fraction of August's crowd.

On July and August weekends the access points and the earth car parks fill early. If you're going then, go in the morning.

## The honest part

Lagoa de Albufeira is a sensitive natural system that takes a great many people in one month of the year. The car parks are packed earth, the accesses are narrow and the facilities are few for August's numbers.

Take your rubbish home. That sounds like throwaway guidebook advice, but in a lagoon that depends on water renewal to stay viable it isn't: here you can see the difference.

And if you're going with children for the first time, find out the state of the bar before choosing where to put the towel.
