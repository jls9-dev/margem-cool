The Mercado do Livramento is Setúbal's municipal market and, at the same time, the reason people eat well in the city. It is not an attraction staged for visitors: it is where Setúbal's restaurants buy their fish in the morning.

First the essentials, because this is where most people go wrong.

## Hours

**Closed on Mondays.** On the other days it is a morning market: it opens early, around seven, and shuts at lunchtime. It also closes on a handful of public holidays through the year.

Two practical consequences. First: if you planned your Setúbal trip for a Monday, the market is not part of it. Second: **go in the morning, and early.** The fish hall is a morning operation and by early afternoon it is being dismantled. Arriving after one is arriving at the end.

An honest note on the times: the sources that publish the Livramento's hours agree on 7am to 2pm, but the council publishes nothing we can re-read. If your visit depends on half an hour, check the market's own page before leaving.

## The fish

Setúbal has its own auction at the mouth of the Sado, and the market is where that catch arrives. That is the difference between the Livramento and any municipal market: here the fish is not a section within a market, it is the axis of the thing.

You see the effect in the city around it. Much of Setúbal's restaurant trade — including the places making the choco frito the city is known for — buys here in the morning, and the day's menu comes out of what there was.

## The tiles

The building is from **1930** and the decoration is of its period: large tile panels from the thirties and forties covering whole interior surfaces, with scenes of fishing, of work and of maritime life.

There is work by **Pedro Pinto**, dated 1929, and panels by **Rosa Rodrigues** from 1944. Unlike much Portuguese tilework, which you meet on façades and in churches, this sits inside a working building with stalls in front of it and people buying fish — which is precisely what makes it interesting.

## How to use the market

If you are coming as a visitor rather than to shop:

- **Go early**, between eight and eleven. That is when it is properly working.
- **Walk the fish first**, then everything else. It closes soonest and it is what justifies the visit.
- **Look up, and at the walls.** The panels go unnoticed by anyone who only looks at the stalls.
- **Eat afterwards, nearby.** The market sells; what you eat in Setúbal came from here this morning.

## The honest part

The Livramento is a working market, not a museum. That means wet floors, crates going past, people at work, and little patience for anyone planted in the middle of an aisle with a camera. It is better that way — the day it stops being that, it stops being worth the visit.

And if you go in August, mind the heat after eleven: the building is from 1930 and you can tell.
