If one dish belongs to the Margem Sul, it is this one. Choco frito is Setúbal's, dozens of places in the city cook it, and the argument about who does it best is a local pastime with decades behind it.

It is worth understanding what it is before ordering — because the difference between a well-made choco frito and a bad one is enormous, and you notice it on the first bite.

## What it is

**Choco** is cuttlefish. Not squid and not octopus: a broader, thicker body with an internal bone, and firmer flesh. It comes from the Sado and the coast in front, and it is because the cuttlefish is from here that the dish is from here.

The dish is simple to describe: cuttlefish cut up, seasoned, floured or battered, fried, served with chips and lemon. No sauce, and none needed.

## Why it goes rubbery, and how it doesn't

This is the part that matters, because it is where places separate themselves from one another.

**The cut.** Neither too thick, which leaves it raw inside and tough outside, nor thin enough to dry out in the fryer.

**The seasoning.** The cuttlefish is marinated first — garlic nearly always, and most places use white wine or lemon. This is the step that softens the flesh, and it is what separates choco frito from cuttlefish that has merely been fried.

**Time in the oil.** Properly hot, briefly. The longer it stays, the tougher it gets. It is fast frying — which is why the good places fry to order rather than keeping trays waiting.

When choco frito comes out rubbery, it nearly always failed here.

## What goes with it

Chips, typically hand-cut, and lemon to squeeze. Tomato rice or a simple salad appear on many menus.

To drink: a cold white from the Setúbal peninsula, or a lager. It is fried food and wants a cold, light drink. Moscatel is for the end of the meal, not alongside.

## How to choose the place

We are not going to give you a list of the five best, because there is no consensus in Setúbal and pretending otherwise would not be honest. What we can tell you is **what to look for**:

- **Places where choco frito is the dish**, not one line among thirty. The ones that live off it treat it better.
- **Frying to order.** If it arrives too fast to have been fried now, it probably wasn't.
- **The room at lunchtime.** Full of people from the city on a weekday is the most reliable sign there is.
- **Be wary of the translation.** A menu with photographs and five languages is rarely where locals eat.

## Where else

Outside Setúbal, choco frito turns up across the Margem Sul and in Lisbon, with very variable quality. The rule of thumb: the further from the Sado, the fewer the guarantees — not out of parochialism, but because the freshness of the cuttlefish is half the dish.
