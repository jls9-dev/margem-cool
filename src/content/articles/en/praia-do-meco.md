The Meco is the opposite of the Arrábida. Where the hills give still-water coves facing south, this coast gives wide sand facing west, the full Atlantic swell, and cliff behind it.

It sits in the Sesimbra concelho, by **Aldeia do Meco**, on the stretch between Lagoa de Albufeira and Fonte da Telha.

## The sea

This is Atlantic with nothing sheltering it. The swell arrives straight, the currents can be strong, and the water is cold even in August.

That makes it good for surfing and demands respect for swimming: stay in the supervised areas, watch the flag, and don't assume that because the Arrábida is thirty minutes away the sea is similar. It isn't.

## The naturist tradition

The Meco has one of the oldest naturist traditions in Portugal, practised mainly on the northern stretch around **Moinho de Baixo**.

It is worth stating plainly because it causes confusion in both directions: **it is not exclusively a naturist beach** — most of the sand is in ordinary use — and the practice is concentrated on well-known stretches. The people who go there know where they are.

## What else is nearby

To the north, **[Lagoa de Albufeira](/en/guides/lagoa-de-albufeira/)**, where the lagoon and the open sea sit two hundred metres apart — the obvious alternative on a big-sea day.

In the village, fish restaurants, some of them decades old.

To the south, on the other side of the concelho, **[Cabo Espichel](/en/guides/cabo-espichel/)**.

## The honest part

The access roads are packed earth and they fill in the morning in August. There is no promenade and no run of concessions, and that is what a lot of people come here for.

On a big-sea day the Meco is not a swimming beach — it is a looking beach. On those days, cross to the lagoon.
