Queijo de Azeitão is sheep's milk, raw, set with **thistle** rather than animal rennet. That is the essential fact, and it explains almost everything else: the soft paste, the slightly bitter finish, and why it isn't sliced.

It carries a protected designation of origin and is made around Azeitão, in the Setúbal concelho, between the Serra da Arrábida and Palmela.

## How it's eaten

**Cut the lid off.** The top comes away as a disc, and you eat the inside with a spoon or spread it on bread.

You do not slice it. A queijo de Azeitão that cuts cleanly into slices is dry, out of condition, or not what it says it is. This is the mistake everyone makes on first meeting it.

## The thistle

The coagulant is the flower of the thistle — *Cynara cardunculus* — rather than animal rennet. It is the technique that defines the family of Portuguese buttery cheeses, of which Azeitão is one, alongside Serra da Estrela.

It gives the soft paste and the faint bitterness at the back of the mouth, which is what separates a cheese of this family from an ordinary sheep's cheese.

## Azeitão and Serra da Estrela

They are cousins and they get confused. Both sheep, both thistle, both soft.

**Serra da Estrela** comes from its mountains and is usually larger. **Azeitão** is smaller, from the Setúbal peninsula, and the ratio of rind to paste often makes it the more intense of the two.

People who like one usually like the other. People who say they are the same thing have not eaten them side by side.

## What to drink

A **castelão red** from Palmela, which is the neighbour, or **moscatel de Setúbal** if you are already at the end of the meal. It is a salty, intense cheese — it does not sit well with a light white.

## Where to buy it

In **Azeitão**, at the dairies and pastry shops in the village — which also make the [torta de Azeitão](/en/places/setubal/azeitao/), the other product of the place.

In Setúbal, at the **[Mercado do Livramento](/en/guides/mercado-do-livramento/)**, in the morning and not on a Monday.

**Buy by producer rather than by brand**, and ask when it was made. A cheese a few weeks old is a different thing from one at two months, and the preference is personal.

## The honest part

It is expensive, and the price has reasons: raw sheep's milk, limited production, and a maturation that cannot be hurried.

It also divides people. The bitterness of the thistle and the intensity are not universally liked, and anyone expecting something mild is surprised. Taste before buying a whole one if you can — in the village, nearly every dairy will let you.
