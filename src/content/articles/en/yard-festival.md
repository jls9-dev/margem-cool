Four days of electronic music and art installations at Quinta do Anjo, at the entrance to the Serra da Arrábida — that's Yard Festival, which in a few years has become, according to trade press, the fastest-growing electronic music festival in Portugal.

For anyone living in Palmela or Azeitão, what matters is less the lineup and more what happens around it — where exactly it sits, what changes locally during those days, and whether there are still tickets.

## What it is and where it sits

The main site is called **White Sand Mountains** — a name that describes the Arrábida's pale limestone landscape — on Avenida António Xavier de Lima, in Quinta do Anjo, a freguesia of the Palmela concelho. The 2026 edition opened with a separate Thursday event at the **Castelo de Palmela**, before the main programme moved to the Quinta do Anjo site from Friday to Sunday.

In 2026, for the first time, the festival expanded from three days to four — 21 to 24 May — with names including Bonobo, Jamie Jones, Seth Troxler, Chris Stussy, Monolink and RY X. The organisers described it as the largest edition to date.

## 2027: dates announced, tickets sold out

Yard's own site points to the next edition on **27, 28, 29 and 30 May 2027**, already marked as sold out. As of the date we checked this, there's no official waitlist or resale route advertised.

One inconsistency is worth recording: the site's own events page still shows, in places, the 2026 dates without having updated them. We've treated the homepage, which clearly states 2027, as the more reliable source — but anyone planning around these dates should check the official site again nearer the time, rather than booking accommodation or travel on this article alone.

## What changes in Quinta do Anjo and Azeitão during the festival

Quinta do Anjo is normally a quiet rural area — vineyards, farms, narrow roads, the pace of a freguesia in the interior of the Palmela concelho. Across the festival's four days, that changes visibly:

- **Traffic.** The roads into Quinta do Anjo and Azeitão carry more traffic than usual, especially in the late afternoon as most of the crowd arrives by car.
- **Accommodation.** Demand for local accommodation rises sharply over those days — anyone with a house or room to let in the area feels this directly.
- **Noise.** Sound from the site is audible in the nearest localities in the late afternoon and through the evening, across the festival's four days.
- **Local trade.** For the area's cafés, grocers and restaurants, it's also the busiest stretch of the year — the practical flip side of the traffic and noise.

## Getting there, if you're going

The festival doesn't advertise its own dedicated transport. The Lisbon–Setúbal Fertagus line has stations at Palmela and Venda do Alcaide, a few kilometres from Quinta do Anjo, and Carris Metropolitana buses run from the Setúbal transport interface. But without a festival shuttle of its own, most people travelling from outside the region arrive by car, via the A2/IC32 with the Palmela exit.

## The honest bit

An international festival drawing several thousand people, built for four days in a freguesia of vineyards and farms in the Palmela interior, is not always an easy fit — it wasn't designed with the people living around the site in mind. But it's also, for local trade, one of the few times of year Quinta do Anjo and Azeitão get the concentrated attention and footfall that usually goes elsewhere in the region. Neither side cancels the other out — it's the cost and the upside of having one of the country's fastest-growing festivals on your doorstep.
