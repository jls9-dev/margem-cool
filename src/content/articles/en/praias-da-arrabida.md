The Arrábida looks nothing like the rest of the Portuguese coast. It's a limestone ridge falling straight into the sea, facing south into the bay of Setúbal — and because the hills shelter it from the Atlantic swell, the water sits still, shallow and clear in a way it simply doesn't on the other side of the peninsula.

There are five main beaches, all inside the Parque Natural da Arrábida, all free to enter. And there's one thing to know before you leave the house in summer: **the car doesn't get through.**

## Arrábida sem Carros: what changes from June to September

From **4 June to 15 September**, every day, **from 7am to 8pm**, private cars are barred from the beach roads. This isn't a suggestion or a heavy-traffic warning: the roads close.

It runs in two zones.

**Eastern zone — Albarquel and Figueirinha.** The closure runs between Travessa da Casa da Saúde, by the Saboaria, and Praia da Figueirinha. The Figueirinha car park is reserved for beach concession staff, motorcycles, and vehicles with a reduced-mobility permit.

**Western zone — Portinho, Creiro, Galapos and Galapinhos.** Access is restricted from the cruzamento do Pinheiro junction on the ER379-1. The road closes whenever the Creiro car park and the authorised bays fill, or when the authorities judge there's a risk.

Outside that period — and outside those hours — you drive to the beaches as normal.

## Getting there without a car

Four bus lines serve the beaches, all covered by the **navegante pass**:

- **4474 — Alegro to Figueirinha.** The most direct route. In July and August it runs an intensive timetable, dozens of trips a day each way, departing from both Alegro and the Várzea terminal.
- **4470 — Setúbal to Creiro, via Azeitão.** Serves the western side.
- **4471 — Albarquel circular.** The beach closest to the city.
- **4477 — free shuttle** between the Creiro car park and Praia de Galapos.

If you drive as far as Creiro, the car park holds around 140 cars and costs **€5 before 1pm, €3 after**, a single rate. It fills early on August weekends, and when it fills the road closes behind you.

## The beaches, one by one

### Figueirinha

The widest sand in the hills and the easiest of the lot. The water deepens very slowly — you can walk a long way out and still be waist-deep — which makes it the obvious choice with children. Across the bay you can see the Tróia sandspit.

It's also the busiest, and it shows in August.

### Galapos and Galapinhos

Smaller, tucked further between the cliffs, with deeper and bluer water. Galapinhos turns up regularly in lists of the country's best beaches, which has had the predictable effect on how many people go.

You reach them on the shuttle from Creiro, or on foot along the trails.

### Portinho da Arrábida

The postcard: a small cove, whitewashed fishermen's houses right at the water, and the Convento da Arrábida on the slope above. It also has the Forte de Santa Maria, now an oceanographic museum.

It's the most photographed and the hardest to park at. Take the bus.

### Creiro

Next to the car park and the campsite. It's the most practical of the five — if you're driving and want the shortest walk, this is it.

### Albarquel

The closest to Setúbal, effectively in the city, with an urban park alongside. It doesn't have the clear water of the others, but you can walk or take the circular from the centre.

## The honest part

The Arrábida is a protected ridge with narrow roads and small beaches, and it receives far more people than the geography holds. The access restrictions exist because of that, not out of bureaucracy — before them, an August day was a queue of parked cars sitting on top of a nature reserve.

What that means in practice: **in August, plan**. Take the bus, or go very early, or go outside the season. In September, after the 15th, the hills give you water that's still warm, free-moving traffic again, and a fraction of the people.

The rules change from year to year. Check the current programme on the Câmara Municipal de Setúbal site before driving down.
