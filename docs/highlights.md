# Margem Cool — highlights

The coolest things about this site. Marketing fuel; keep it honest.

## The weekly fact check
Any page stating something perishable — an opening time, a fare, a festival date, an access rule — declares that fact and its source in frontmatter. Every Monday the source is re-read and drift is reported. **Why it's cool:** guides across the internet still tell people to catch the Transpraia beach train, which stopped running in 2019. This site is built so that can't happen to it. Nothing else in the portfolio does this yet.

## The queue knows *when*, not just *what*
Beach terms swing 25× between February and August; Carnaval de Sesimbra swings 220×. A page needs months to rank, so the queue ranks by peak month minus four rather than by annual average. **Why it's cool:** it found that ameijoas à Bulhão Pato and moscatel de Setúbal peak at Christmas, so they get written in August — which no one would have guessed.

## Ferry fares that read themselves
`npm run fares` reads the operator's own tariff table into dated JSON. **Why it's cool:** it turned up a fact the pages were missing — paying with zapping crosses to Lisbon at €1.60 against €2.00 for a single ticket.

## The interactive Margem Sul map
Real GeoJSON for all nine concelhos on CARTO tiles, clickable through to each place. **Why it's cool:** it is the region's actual shape, not a stylised approximation, and it is the front door to the whole site.

## Photographs that are ours
Place and guide heroes come from a real Margem Sul library — the Cais do Ginjal pier in fog, Ponto Final's yellow tables on the quay, the Cristo Rei silhouette at sunset. **Why it's cool:** most regional guides run stock. Where we have no photograph we use a properly licensed one and name the photographer, and where neither exists we ship without rather than run something poor.

## The honest section
Every guide ends with what is genuinely difficult, contested or overrated about its subject — the Cacilhas waterfront being neither working port nor finished promenade, Cabo Espichel's pilgrim houses shut for decades. **Why it's cool:** it is the opposite of tourism copy, and it is why the pages read as written by someone who lives there.
