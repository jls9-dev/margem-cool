The Serra da Arrábida is why this part of the Margem Sul looks nothing like the rest of the country. It is a ridge of **limestone** running east to west between Setúbal and Cabo Espichel, falling straight into the sea on its southern face.

Everything that makes the Arrábida different comes from that.

## Why the water is different

This is the useful thing to understand before choosing a beach.

The Arrábida beaches are on the **south side of the ridge**, facing into the bay of Setúbal. The hills sit between them and the Atlantic, and cut the swell. The result is still, shallow, clear water.

Thirty minutes away, the **Costa da Caparica** faces west, straight into the Atlantic, with nothing sheltering it. Wide sand, real swell, cold water.

Two different seas on one peninsula, and the ridge is the explanation.

## The road

The **hill road**, between Setúbal and the Portinho da Arrábida, is the best drive in the region. It runs along the slope with the bay always below, and has viewpoints where stopping is genuinely worth it.

From **4 June to 15 September**, **7am to 8pm**, beach access is restricted — the road itself still exists, but the descents to the sand close. The rules are in the [beaches guide](/en/guides/praias-da-arrabida/).

## The Convento da Arrábida

On the slope above the Portinho, a **sixteenth-century Franciscan convent**, with cells scattered across the hillside rather than gathered into one building — the friars lived apart from one another, with the hills and the sea in front of them.

Visiting is restricted and by appointment. It is not an open-door monument, and it is worth knowing that before driving up expecting one.

## The vegetation

What covers the hills is **Mediterranean maquis** — kermes oak, strawberry tree, wild olive, mastic — and it is rare at a European scale. It survived here because the slope is too steep to plough, and it has been protected since 1976 by the [Parque Natural da Arrábida](/en/guides/parque-natural-da-arrabida/).

In spring the hills flower and change colour. It is the best time to walk, and the one fewest people choose.

## Trails

There are marked routes across the hills and along the slope, including those linking Creiro to Galapos and [Galapinhos](/en/guides/praia-dos-galapinhos/).

They are exposed: little shade, reflective limestone, and serious heat in summer. Take water and don't do this at midday in August.

## The honest part

The Arrábida is small. On a map it looks like a mountain range; in practice you cross it by car in twenty minutes, and the visitor pressure it takes in August is out of proportion to its size.

The traffic restriction exists because of that, and it works. But the August experience — bus, queue, full beach — is not the same thing as September, when you can drive the road slowly, stop at the viewpoints and have Galapinhos more or less to yourself.

If you can choose, choose out of season.
