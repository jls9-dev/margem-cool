Castelo de Sesimbra stands above the town, and it is **free**. That, and the wide opening hours, probably make it the best thing you can do in the concelho without spending anything.

It is also, in the council's own words, **the last of the Portuguese castles over the sea to keep its medieval form**. It was classified a National Monument on **16 June 1910**.

## The essentials

**Free entry.**

**Summer:** every day, **10am to 8pm**.
**Winter:** every day, **7am to 7pm**.

## What's inside

More than most people expect from a castle with free entry.

**The walls are walkable**, and that is what you come for: the view takes in the town below, the sheltered bay and the open sea beyond it.

Within the grounds there are **two permanent exhibitions** on the monument's history, a **heritage interpretation centre**, a **church** and a **disused cemetery**. A museum circuit lets you work through it all on your own, without a guided visit.

There is a **café with a terrace**, serving some local products.

## The history, briefly

The origins are **ninth-century**, from the period of Muslim occupation.

**1165** — Afonso Henriques takes it.
**1191** — it is lost to the Almohads.
**1199** — Sancho I retakes it definitively and grants the town its first charter, with Sesimbra then sitting **inside the castle itself**.

That last part changes how you look at the place: the town of Sesimbra began up here, behind the walls, and only later came down to the seafront where it is today.

Through the Middle Ages the castle was repeatedly enlarged, given its importance in defending the kingdom's sea and land frontiers.

## When to go

**Late in the day.** The town faces west and the light lands on the white houses before the sun sets into the sea.

Early morning is the other good hour — the winter timetable opens at 7am, unusually early for a monument.

## The honest part

The walk up from the town is a real gradient. Most people drive, and in August the parking by the castle fills.

And it is worth stating the obvious: this is a castle, not a climate-controlled museum. It is exposed to wind and sun, and there is no shade on the walls.
